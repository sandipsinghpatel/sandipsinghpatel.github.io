Our study of exosomes released by senescent human cells, and of circulating exosomes isolated from the plasma of younger and older adults, is now published in *Aging*.
<!--more-->

Using data-independent-acquisition proteomics together with lipidomics, we characterised the cargo of extracellular vesicles from senescent fibroblasts and epithelial cells and compared it with vesicles isolated from human plasma. A core set of proteins and lipid classes changed consistently with senescence and with donor age, pointing to vesicle cargo as both a mechanism of senescence spread and a source of accessible biomarkers of biological age.

The work was carried out with Birgit Schilling, Judith Campisi, Erin Baker and colleagues at the Buck Institute for Research on Aging and North Carolina State University.

Read the paper: [Patel *et al.*, *Aging* 2025; 17: 1929–1965](https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.206292) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40748658/)
