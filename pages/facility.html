We provide quantitative mass-spectrometry support to groups in the MRC Toxicology Unit, across the University of Cambridge, and to external academic and industrial partners. Most projects reach us as a biological question rather than a finished sample set — and that is the best time to talk.

Discuss a proteomics project

What can we measure?

Global proteomics

Deep, quantitative profiling by data-independent acquisition (DIA) or DDA, with label-free quantification for most designs.

Multiplexed quantification

TMT labelling for tightly controlled comparisons across many conditions or time points in one batch.

Specialised proteomes

Secretome, extracellular-vesicle and cell-surface proteomics, and post-translational-modification enrichment.

Targeted assays

PRM methods for sensitive, confident quantification of a defined protein panel across large sample series.

What samples are suitable?

Cultured cells, primary and iPSC-derived cells, tissue (fresh, frozen or FFPE), conditioned medium, isolated extracellular vesicles, plasma and serum, and CSF. Samples should be snap-frozen or supplied in a buffer compatible with mass spectrometry — avoid detergents, high salt and polymers such as PEG unless agreed with us in advance. If in doubt, ask before you collect: the wrong buffer is the most common reason a promising sample set cannot be analysed.

How many replicates?

Four to six biological replicates per group is the usual starting point for discovery proteomics, and is what most designs need to detect biologically meaningful differences. Technical replicates rarely add value once biological replication is adequate. We are glad to review a power calculation with you.

Instrumentation

Acquisition is performed on a Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Eclipse Tribrid mass spectrometer with FAIMS Pro interface, coupled to nanoflow liquid chromatography, with automated sample preparation. Data are processed in Spectronaut, DIA-NN and MaxQuant, with statistics and visualisation in R. Every batch carries system-suitability standards and quality-control metrics are tracked across acquisition series, so drift is caught before it reaches your data.

What you receive

  • Processed protein- and peptide-level quantification, with the raw files
  • A quality-control report for the acquisition series
  • Differential-abundance statistics and publication-ready figures
  • A meeting to work through what the data mean and what to do next
  • Support with data deposition to PRIDE/ProteomeXchange at publication

Project workflow

  1. Enquiry. Email a short description of your system, the comparison you want to make, and the number of conditions and replicates.
  2. Design meeting. We agree sample type and amount, replication, quantification strategy, expected depth, approximate cost and realistic turnaround.
  3. Sample preparation. In the facility, or in your laboratory to a protocol we provide.
  4. Acquisition and QC. Samples randomised across the run order with standards interleaved; QC reviewed before any results are released.
  5. Analysis and handover. Processed data, statistics, figures and a discussion of next steps.

Training

We train PhD students, postdoctoral researchers and visiting scientists in sample preparation, instrument operation, quality control and data analysis, one-to-one and through workshops — particularly for groups planning to adopt proteomics as a routine method rather than a one-off service request.
