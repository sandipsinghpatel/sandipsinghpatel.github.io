The Proteomics Facility provides quantitative mass-spectrometry support to research groups within the MRC Toxicology Unit, across the University of Cambridge, and to external academic and industrial partners. We are a full-service facility: most projects come to us as a biological question rather than a finished sample set, and we help shape the experiment before any material is consumed.

What we offer

A.

Discovery proteomics

Deep, quantitative profiling by data-independent acquisition (DIA) or data-dependent acquisition (DDA), with label-free quantification for most study designs.

B.

Multiplexed quantification

TMT and iTRAQ labelling for tightly controlled comparisons across many conditions or time points within a single acquisition batch.

C.

Targeted assays

PRM and SRM methods for confident, sensitive quantification of a defined protein or peptide panel across large sample series.

D.

Specialised workflows

Secretomics, extracellular-vesicle and cell-surface proteomics, post-translational-modification enrichment, thermal-proteome profiling and low-input samples.

Instrumentation

Acquisition is performed on Thermo Scientific Orbitrap Exploris 480 and Orbitrap Fusion platforms coupled to nanoflow liquid chromatography. Data are processed in Spectronaut, DIA-NN and MaxQuant, with downstream statistics and visualisation in R. Every batch carries system-suitability standards, and quality-control metrics are tracked across acquisition series so that drift is detected before it reaches your data.

Starting a project

  1. Get in touch early. Email a short description of your system, the comparison you want to make and the number of conditions and replicates. A ten-minute conversation at this stage routinely saves months.
  2. Feasibility and design. We advise on sample type and minimum amount, replication, the appropriate quantification strategy, expected depth of coverage, approximate cost and realistic turnaround.
  3. Sample preparation. Prepared either in the facility or in your own laboratory to a protocol we provide, depending on the workflow and your experience.
  4. Acquisition and quality control. Samples are randomised across the run order, with standards interleaved; we review QC before releasing any results.
  5. Analysis and interpretation. You receive processed protein- and peptide-level data, a statistical summary, and figures — plus a meeting to work through what they mean and what to do next.

Training

We train PhD students, postdoctoral researchers and visiting scientists in sample preparation, instrument operation, quality control and data analysis, both one-to-one and through workshops. If your group is planning to take on proteomics as a routine method rather than a one-off service request, that is exactly the situation the training is designed for.
