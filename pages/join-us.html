We are looking for careful, curious scientists who want to work where mass spectrometry, cell biology and ageing research meet. You do not need to arrive as a proteomics expert — we train every member in sample preparation, instrument operation, quality control and data analysis — but you do need to enjoy quantitative thinking, and to be the kind of person who asks whether a result would survive being measured a second way.

In practice, most projects here pair a biological question with a methodological one. You might spend a year learning to measure the secretome of a few thousand sorted microglia reliably, because that is what the biology demands. We think that combination makes for unusually employable scientists, in academia and beyond.

Current opportunities

A.

Postdoctoral researchers

We are glad to support fellowship applications — Marie Skłodowska-Curie, EMBO, HFSP, Wellcome Early Career, Newton International, and BBSRC/MRC schemes among them — from candidates with a background in proteomics, neuroscience, cell biology or computational biology. Please write well before the deadline: a competitive application needs several weeks of joint work on the proposal.

B.

PhD students

Prospective students apply through the University of Cambridge and the MRC Toxicology Unit's PhD programme. Projects are available combining proteomics with hiPSC-derived neural models or extracellular-vesicle biology. Get in touch before you apply so we can shape a project around your interests.

C.

Visiting scientists and interns

Short visits of three to twelve months, for researchers who want to learn DIA proteomics, EV isolation, sample preparation or data analysis, are welcome subject to space and funding. Master's and undergraduate placements are considered case by case.

What we offer

  1. Training on Orbitrap Exploris 480 and Fusion instruments, from sample to statistics, with enough independence that you can design your own experiments by the end of your first year.
  2. Projects positioned between mechanism and translation, so your work has both a biological story and a measurable readout.
  3. An international collaborative network across the UK, USA, Japan and India, and support to present your work at ISEV, HUPO, ASMS and equivalent meetings.
  4. Honest, regular supervision — including on career paths outside academia, which are a legitimate destination rather than a failure mode.

Collaborate with the Proteomics Facility

If you are a Cambridge or external researcher with a proteomics question, we can advise on feasibility, experimental design, minimum sample requirements, quantification strategy, approximate cost and realistic turnaround. Email proteomics@mrc-tox.cam.ac.uk with a short description of your samples and aims, or see the facility page.

How to apply

Send a single PDF containing your CV, a one-page statement explaining your research interests and why this laboratory in particular, and the names of two referees, to skp59@cam.ac.uk, with “Application — [position]” in the subject line. We reply to every application, and we are happy to talk informally before you write anything formal.
