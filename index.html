We use quantitative mass spectrometry to understand how senescent cells, inflammation and extracellular vesicles shape the ageing brain — and how those signals might be measured and modified.

Ageing across biological length scales

We are a mass-spectrometry proteomics group led by Dr Sandip Kumar Patel at the MRC Toxicology Unit, University of Cambridge. Our subject is cellular senescence: cells that stop dividing with age but keep talking, secreting proteins, lipids and vesicles that inflame the tissue around them.

We follow that conversation from the cell that starts it to the plasma sample in which it might be detected — profiling the secretome and vesicle cargo of senescent cells, asking how senescent glia change neurons in human iPSC-derived models of the ageing brain, and testing which of those signals survive as measurable markers of biological age. The aim is to connect a mechanism worth targeting with a measurement worth making.

Sandip also directs the Unit's Proteomics Facility, which provides DDA, DIA, TMT and targeted LC-MS/MS workflows, reproducible quality control, data analysis and hands-on training to researchers across Cambridge and beyond.
